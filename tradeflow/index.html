Concepts covered.

International Trade

Gravity Model of Trade

The gravity model predicts bilateral trade flows. Trade volume ∝ (GDP_A × GDP_B) / Distance.

  • What GDP and distance do to trade volume
  • Why large, nearby economies trade more
  • How to interpret the trade index
International Trade

Tariffs & Trade Policy

A tariff is a tax on imported goods. TradeFlow covers four real-world tariff examples — including a 778% rice tariff.

  • What makes a tariff "ridiculous"
  • How tariffs distort trade flows
  • Historical context for real tariffs
International Trade

Autarky — Can It Exist?

Autarky is full national self-sufficiency — no imports, no exports. In practice, no true autarky has ever been sustained.

  • What autarky means in trade theory
  • Albania's near-autarky experiment
  • Post-scarcity as the only viable scenario
Management Science

Linear Programming

LP finds the best outcome subject to constraints. TradeFlow lets you build and solve LP problems visually.

  • Standard LP notation
  • Writing objective functions and constraints
  • Graphical solution and feasible region
  • Corner point theorem

Logistics

TLC vs SCM

The TLC covers internal logistics flow. SCM wraps around it, including suppliers and customers.

  • The four stages of the TLC

It implements the basic gravity model formula: T = A × (GDP_A × GDP_B) / D. This is the standard textbook version. Output is a trade potential index, not actual dollar value.
It uses the corner point theorem — the optimal solution always occurs at a corner of the feasible region. The tool enumerates all corner points and returns the one with highest profit.
The TLC describes internal logistics flow. SCM is broader — it includes the supplier at the start and the customer at the end, both external to the TLC.
