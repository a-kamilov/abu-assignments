01 — Foundations

External Economies of Scale

When many firms from the same industry gather in one location, the whole becomes greater than the sum of its parts. Costs fall. Productivity rises.

Specialized Suppliers

A dense cluster attracts suppliers who specialize in serving that exact industry. Components become readily available at lower cost.

Labor Market Pooling

Firms collectively train and attract a deep pool of specialized workers. Hiring is faster, skills sharper.

Knowledge Spillovers

Ideas travel fast when engineers work near each other. Innovation at one firm spills into neighbors.

What Is an Industrial Cluster?

A geographic concentration of interconnected companies. Silicon Valley, London's financial district, Qiaotou's button factories — each benefits from a self-reinforcing cycle: more firms attract more suppliers and workers, which attracts more firms. These advantages are external to any single firm.

04 — Deep Dive

Qiaotou, China:
The Button Capital of the World

A small town in Zhejiang Province now supplies approximately 60% of the world's buttons.

From Rural Town to Global Supplier

In the 1980s, a handful of entrepreneurs began manufacturing simple plastic buttons. Today Qiaotou produces over 15 billion buttons annually — not because land or labor is cheapest there, but because the cluster itself generates advantages no new entrant can replicate.
