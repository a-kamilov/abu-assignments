Project Overview

This project was inspired by a classroom activity exploring unusual and strict tariff policies in Uzbekistan. We examined how trade restrictions affect ordinary consumers, local industries, and market competition.

A tariff is a tax or duty placed by a government on imported goods. When a country imports a product, the government charges the importer a percentage of the product's value before it can be sold. This makes foreign goods more expensive, encouraging people to buy locally made products instead. While tariffs can protect local industries and jobs, they can also reduce consumer choice and raise prices significantly.

💸
How Tariffs Affect Prices

Tariffs are added directly to the cost of imported goods. Importers pass these costs to consumers, meaning the product on the shelf can cost far more than it would without the tax.

🛒
Effect on Consumers

Consumers face fewer imported choices and higher prices. In extreme cases, like cars in Uzbekistan, imported goods can become 2–3× more expensive, making them unaffordable for most people.

🏭
Protecting Local Industry

High tariffs shield domestic producers from foreign competition. Uzbekistan uses this to protect its local car manufacturer (GM Uzbekistan / UzAuto) and support domestic employment.

⚖️
Trade-offs & Debates

Protectionism creates winners (local producers) and losers (consumers). Critics argue it reduces innovation and quality, while supporters say it's necessary for developing economies to build industries.

03

Tariff Examples

🚗

Foreign Imported Cars

Extreme customs duties make imported vehicles 2–3× more expensive

🏭 Protecting Domestic Industry

Uzbekistan imposes some of the highest car import tariffs in Central Asia. Importing a foreign vehicle involves stacking multiple fees: customs duties of roughly 30%, an excise tax around 15%, VAT at 15%, plus registration charges. The combined effect can push the final price to two or even three times the original manufacturer's suggested retail price.

The stated goal is to protect UzAuto Motors (formerly GM Uzbekistan), the dominant domestic car producer. With local car production supported by the government, high tariffs keep foreign competition at bay. While this preserves local jobs and industrial capacity, consumers are left with fewer choices and significantly higher prices — and often lower quality vehicles than those available in other markets.

30%+ Customs duty on most foreign cars
2–3× Final price vs. original price
~85% UzAuto's market share domestically

💛

Gold & Jewelry Import Restrictions

Strict government control over gold trade and personal jewelry imports

🚫 State Control Policy

Uzbekistan maintains strict government control over its gold reserves and gold trade. Citizens and travelers are severely restricted in how much gold or jewelry they can bring into or out of the country without declaration or heavy taxation. Bringing more than a small personal allowance of gold jewelry across the border requires customs declaration and, in many cases, paying substantial import taxes.

The government justifies this policy as a way to protect national wealth and maintain economic control, particularly since Uzbekistan is one of the world's top gold-producing countries. The state gold reserve and the Central Bank's gold holdings are considered strategic assets. However, this creates an uncomfortable situation for ordinary citizens who may have legally purchased jewelry abroad and find themselves taxed on their personal belongings when returning home.

💊

Medicine Import Tax

New 2025 customs duty on antibiotics, vitamins, and painkillers

📋 New 2025 Policy

Beginning in 2025, Uzbekistan introduced a customs duty of approximately 2% on a wide range of imported medicines, including common antibiotics, vitamins, and over-the-counter painkillers. This policy was introduced alongside stricter regulations on the quantity and type of medicines travelers may bring into the country personally.

While 2% may sound small, when combined with VAT, distributor markups, and regulatory compliance costs, the final price increase for consumers can be significant — particularly for essential medications that people rely on regularly. Critics argue that taxing life-saving medicines is fundamentally different from taxing luxury goods, as it directly impacts public health outcomes. Patients who depend on specific imported medicines may find them harder to access or afford after the policy change.

~2% New customs duty rate (2025)
Broad Covers antibiotics, vitamins, painkillers

What Did I Learn?

"Tariffs are not just numbers on a government document — they are real decisions that change whether families can afford a car, whether a patient can access medicine, and whether a country opens itself to the world or closes its borders."

📖 What I Learned About Tariffs

I learned that tariffs are more complex than simply "taxes on imports." They are policy tools that governments use strategically — sometimes to protect infant industries, sometimes to generate revenue, and sometimes to maintain political control. The Uzbekistan case shows all three motivations playing out simultaneously across cars, gold, and medicine.

👨‍👩‍👧 Impact on Everyday People

The most striking realization was how directly tariffs impact ordinary consumers. A family that wants a reliable foreign car pays double or triple. A patient who needs a specific antibiotic faces higher costs. A traveler returning with jewelry faces bureaucratic hurdles. Tariffs translate government policy into daily hardship for real people.

🛠️ Skills Gained

This activity helped me develop skills in economic research, data analysis, and critical thinking about policy trade-offs. I practiced reading official government documents, cross-referencing news sources, and translating complex tariff schedules into understandable examples. Building the calculators also helped me understand the compounding math behind multiple tax layers.

💭 Personal Reflections

What surprised me most was the medicine tariff. Protecting a car industry with high tariffs is controversial, but understandable as economic policy. But taxing antibiotics and painkillers seems to prioritize revenue over public health. This made me question whether all tariffs deserve the same analysis, or whether essential goods should be protected from protectionism itself.

🌍 Broader Takeaway

Trade policy is ultimately about values: do we value consumer choice, affordability, and global integration — or do we value domestic employment, national self-sufficiency, and economic control? Uzbekistan's tariff choices reflect a government that has historically prioritized the latter. As the country continues its economic reforms begun under President Mirziyoyev, it will be interesting to see whether these "ridiculous" tariffs soften over time as global trade integration becomes more attractive.
