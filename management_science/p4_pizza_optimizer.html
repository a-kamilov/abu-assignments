Project Overview

Pizza Topping Optimization

🎯 Problem Scenario

A pizza party organizer has a budget of 7.00 woowoos. Each topping has a cost and a satisfaction score (1–10). The goal: maximize total satisfaction without exceeding budget. Each topping is either chosen (1) or not (0).

Decision variables: x₁...x₁₀ ∈ {0,1}. Objective function: maximize Σ (satisfactionᵢ × xᵢ). Constraint: Σ (costᵢ × xᵢ) ≤ 7.00. This mimics real-world resource allocation, capital budgeting, and portfolio selection.

📊 Optimization Relevance

Binary LP models appear in project selection, menu planning, and manufacturing. The pizza problem simplifies knapsack-style decisions: managers evaluate trade-offs between benefit (satisfaction) and limited resource (budget).

💡 Key insight: The most expensive topping isn't always optimal — we must balance marginal satisfaction per woowoo.

💡 Conclusion — Why the first solution is optimal despite spending less:
The objective function in this problem is to maximize total satisfaction, not to spend the entire budget. The best solution achieves 26.00 satisfaction units at a cost of only 6.50 woowoos. The second-best solution spends the full 7.00 woowoos but yields only 25.60 satisfaction — a 0.40 point decrease despite spending 0.50 woowoos more.

This demonstrates a core principle of optimization: resource efficiency matters more than resource consumption. The optimal combination selects toppings with the highest satisfaction-to-cost ratio (Salame: 4.15 sat/ww, Nduja: 9.6 sat/ww, Ricotta: 4.2 sat/ww, etc.) rather than simply maximizing spending. Real-world managers should always prioritize marginal benefit per unit of constrained resource.
